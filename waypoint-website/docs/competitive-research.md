October 2026. This brief informed the redesign. Direct visits to the competitor sites were blocked from the build environment, so the findings come from search-engine indexes of their sites, their LinkedIn pages and public business profiles.

## What makes a recruiting firm look big

Every firm above is far smaller than its website suggests. The impression of scale comes from **structure and content, not headcount**:

1. **Discipline verticals.** Each discipline gets its own section or page, with named roles and credentials.
2. **Engagement models.** Contingent, engaged, retained and contract options are presented as a clear menu.
3. **Two-audience navigation.** "Hire Talent" and "Find Jobs" sit side by side, with separate calls to action for each.
4. **Process and guarantees.** A defined search process, plus guarantee language.
5. **Content.** Insights articles, salary guides and market reports.
6. **Candidate care.** A named recruiter, a confidentiality promise, an FAQ, and "free for candidates".
7. **Firm signals.** Office locations, a careers page for recruiters, and an EEO statement.
8. **Proof.** Stats, testimonials and awards. These carry the most weight, so they must be real.

## What the redesign adopted

- Discipline verticals with role lists and credentials (`sectors.html`). We kept an MEP-first angle: the three Orlando firms are broad AEC generalists, and only MEP Staff and Blue Signal go deep on building systems.
- A hiring-models comparison table and "Submit a job order" language (`employers.html#models`).
- A candidate FAQ and "always free for candidates" messaging (`jobs.html#faq`).
- An Insights section with three full articles (`insights.html`).
- A compensation-benchmark offer, which a recruiter can actually deliver, in place of a salary guide that doesn't exist yet.
- A "Careers at Waypoint" section (`about.html#careers`).

## What the redesign deliberately avoided

You asked that the site not publish real clients or real job postings. We also avoided anything that would be invented proof:

- **No client names or logos.** The site shows client types instead ("MEP engineering firms", "General contractors"…).
- **No live job postings.** The jobs page shows *role profiles*: representative roles, typical base ranges and typical employer types, labeled as such. Candidates are matched to confidential live searches.
- **No invented stats.** The stats band shows scope figures that are true as written (8 disciplines, 60+ role types, 4 hiring models, $0 to candidates). The first draft's placeholder "94% retention" happened to match Blue Signal's published figure, which was one more reason to drop it.
- **No invented testimonials.** These were replaced with published service standards. Fabricated testimonials are a real legal liability under the FTC's 2024 rule on fake reviews and testimonials and under deceptive-advertising law generally.

## Recommendations beyond the website

1. **Start tracking real numbers now.** Placements, 12-month retention and days to shortlist are the stats competitors lead with. Even a few months of data makes a credible stat.
2. **Collect real testimonials.** Ask satisfied clients and candidates for permission to quote them, even anonymized by title. A review platform such as Google reviews or ClearlyRated adds third-party credibility.
3. **LinkedIn company page.** Match the website's positioning in the tagline and banner, post Insights articles there, and encourage recruiters to list the company. The three Orlando firms each have only ~1–2k followers, so this gap is closable.
4. **Local and discipline SEO pages.** AE Concepts and KORE1 both use pages like "MEP recruiter in Orlando" or "Electrical engineering recruiter in Texas". These pages are cheap to add and capture search traffic.
5. **Name collisions to be aware of.** Search results surface other "Waypoint" recruiting brands, including Waypoint Recruitment (an architecture and construction recruiter in Canada) and a "Waypoint Search, Inc." LinkedIn page focused on accounting and tech roles. Make sure your LinkedIn and Google Business Profile clearly match waypointsearch.com.
